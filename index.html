1. Expatriate Male Asymmetry

Notice the colossal asymmetry between ages 20 and 39 in the Total view. Non-Maldivian residents have a sex ratio of over 1,017 men per 100 women (162,210 foreign men vs 15,946 women in 2020), primarily employed in resort operations and infrastructure construction.

Foreign Sex Ratio 10.2 : 1 (M:F)
Maldivian Sex Ratio 1.05 : 1 (M:F)

2. Malé Capital Urban Siphon

Toggle between Malé Capital City and Outer Atolls. Young working-age adults (20–34) cluster disproportionately in the capital for higher education and white-collar commerce, leaving the Outer Atolls with a higher child and elderly dependency ratio.

Malé Working-Age (15-64) 81.4%
Outer Atolls Working-Age 68.2%

3. Demographic Dividend & Aging

Switch to the 1995–2014 Census mode. In 1995, children aged 0–14 comprised 47.0% of Maldivians. By 2021, that dropped to 27.7%, expanding the prime-age productive workforce and gradually shifting median age from 16.5 to over 33 years by 2030.

1995 Youth (0–14) 47.0% of population
2030 Youth (0–14) Proj. 24.5% of population
